1. NLP & Normalization

Raw messages contain a lot of noise — capital letters, punctuation, and common joining words like "is", "the", and "for" (known as stopwords). If we feed these directly to an ML model, it will overfit on high-frequency noise.

Our preprocessing pipeline standardizes text:

  • Lowercase: Ensures "CLAIM" and "claim" map to the identical vector index.
  • Tokenization: Splitting clean text blocks into individual semantic tokens.
  • Stopword Removal: Discards language noise words to isolate critical words.
  • Porter Stemming: Reduces suffixes to map related variations (e.g., "winner", "winning", and "won" all stem to "win").

2. TF-IDF Vectorization

Computers do not read words; they read numbers. To bridge this gap, we use **TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency)** to vectorize our textual tokens into numerical weights.

// Mathematical Foundation
TF(t, d) = (Frequency of term t in document d) / (Total terms in d)
IDF(t) = log(Total documents / Documents containing t)
Weight(t, d) = TF(t, d) * IDF(t)

This mechanism ensures that words which appear in almost *every* message get lower weights, while highly distinct terms (like "lottery" or "verify") carry incredibly high threat weights inside the vectorized sparse array.

3. Multinomial Naive Bayes

The classification core relies on **Multinomial Naive Bayes**. It is a probabilistic classifier based on Bayes' Theorem, with the "naive" assumption of conditional independence between every term in the feature vector.

// Bayes' Rule Formula
P(Spam | Message) = [ P(Message | Spam) * P(Spam) ] / P(Message)

Despite its simplifying independence assumptions, Naive Bayes performs exceptionally well in spam classification, outperforming much heavier models due to its resilience against overfitting on high-dimensional text datasets.

4. Cybersecurity Risk Levels

Once Multinomial Naive Bayes predicts class likelihood distributions, we evaluate the threat risk bounds using continuous probability weights:

  • High Risk Class predicted as Spam and probability weight is exceeding 85%. Represents extreme threat indicators, commonly matching OTP fraud attempts or spoof links.
  • Medium Risk Class predicted as Spam and probability weight is **between 60% and 85%**. Flagged due to matching highly suspicious vocabulary clusters.
  • Low Risk Class predicted as Safe, or predicted as Spam but with probability weight **under 60%**. Represents benign regular communication messages.
